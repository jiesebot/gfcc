THE ISSUES

For the city we call home.

I’m pro-worker, pro-union, pro-public education, and committed to privacy, due process, and the neighborhoods that make Green Bay home. Here is where I stand.

01HOUSING & AFFORDABILITY

Homes people can afford.

People should be able to build a life in Green Bay without being priced out of their neighborhoods.

My priorities

  • Cut needless barriers to building homes, including sensible updates to zoning and permits.
  • Support more housing choices: starter homes, apartments, accessible homes, and housing for seniors.
  • Make infrastructure and redevelopment decisions with affordability and existing neighbors in mind.
  • Keep residents informed and involved when development proposals affect their blocks.
A question for neighbors: What would make it easier to find or keep a home in Green Bay?
02SAFE STREETS & ACCESSIBILITY

A city everyone can navigate.

Green Bay should work for everyone, whether they drive, take transit, bike, walk, or use a wheelchair.

My priorities

  • Prioritize safe crossings, accessible curb ramps, sidewalks, and reliable winter maintenance.
  • Fix dangerous intersections and address repair needs using resident feedback and safety information.
  • Support practical transportation connections to jobs, schools, parks, and essential services.
  • Build accessibility into city projects from the start.
A question for neighbors: Which street or public space needs attention?
03WORKERS, UNIONS & GOOD JOBS

Pro-worker. Pro-union.

Every worker deserves dignity on the job. I stand with organized labor and with every worker fighting for fair pay, safe conditions, predictable schedules, and a voice at work.

My priorities

  • Support workers’ right to organize, bargain collectively, and advocate for themselves without intimidation.
  • Use city purchasing and contracting decisions to encourage good jobs, workplace safety, and responsible labor practices.
  • Treat municipal workers fairly, listen to frontline employees, and respect the public services they provide.
  • Speak up for fair pay, reliable hours, benefits, and workplace protections for union and nonunion workers alike.
  • Back locally owned businesses that invest in their employees and neighborhoods.
A question for neighbors: What would make work better for you or your family?
04PUBLIC SCHOOLS & LEARNING

Stand up for public education.

A city that cares about its future cares about students, teachers, support staff, and lifelong learners. Strong public schools are part of a strong Green Bay.

My priorities

  • Be a reliable City Hall partner to public schools, educators, students, and families.
  • Prioritize safe walking routes, crossings, sidewalks, and neighborhood connections near schools.
  • Champion accessible libraries, recreation, youth programming, and places to learn outside the classroom.
  • Support public-school workers, from classroom teachers and aides to custodians, bus drivers, and food-service staff.
  • Make city decisions with the next generation in mind.
A question for neighbors: What city-level support would help students or educators?
05TRANSPARENCY & ACCOUNTABILITY

City Hall should answer to you.

The people of Green Bay deserve clear information, accessible meetings, and a representative who follows through.

My priorities

  • Explain major council votes and their costs in plain language.
  • Make it easier to find public meeting information, records, and opportunities to comment.
  • Offer ways to participate for neighbors who cannot get to a weekday meeting.
  • Respond to constituent concerns and communicate what happens next.
A question for neighbors: What would make local government easier to deal with?
06PRIVACY & CIVIL LIBERTIES

No more Flock.

I oppose Green Bay’s use of Flock Safety’s automated license-plate readers. Public safety matters, and so does everyone’s right to move through their city without routine tracking.

My priorities

  • Push to end the city’s Flock Safety contract and remove its fixed license-plate reader cameras.
  • Oppose expansions of mass-surveillance systems and demand public debate before any new surveillance contracts.
  • Seek full disclosure of who can search local data, who receives it, and what auditing safeguards exist.
  • Support evidence-based public safety strategies that do not depend on routinely collecting the movements of ordinary residents.
A question for neighbors: What privacy protections should Green Bay adopt?
07IMMIGRANT RIGHTS & PUBLIC SAFETY

Oppose ICE. Protect neighbors.

I oppose ICE raids and mass-deportation practices that separate families and undermine trust. Everyone in Green Bay deserves dignity, due process, and the ability to seek help when they need it.

My priorities

  • Oppose using Green Bay staff, equipment, or city funds to carry out civil immigration enforcement.
  • Advocate policies that keep local policing focused on local public safety, not immigration sweeps.
  • Defend residents’ ability to report crime, seek city services, and participate in community life without unnecessary immigration-status questioning.
  • Support clear, multilingual information about residents’ rights and available community resources.
  • Insist on transparency about requests from federal immigration authorities and city responses.
A question for neighbors: How can city services remain accessible and trustworthy for every neighbor?
08WATER, ENERGY & LAND USE

No new mega data centers.

I oppose new hyperscale data-center construction in Green Bay. Public land, water, utility capacity, and taxpayer dollars should serve the people who live and work here.

My priorities

  • Push for zoning changes that prevent new hyperscale data-center developments in Green Bay.
  • Propose a pause on new large-scale applications while the city strengthens land-use standards.
  • Oppose city subsidies, land deals, and infrastructure giveaways for data-center projects.
  • Demand public accounting of proposed projects’ water use, electrical demand, noise, traffic, and long-term costs.
  • Work for stronger local and state protections for residents and ratepayers.
A question for neighbors: What do you want protected when the city considers major industrial projects?
09PARKS, PLACES & LOCAL PRIDE

Invest in places we share.

Our city’s character comes from its parks, riverfront, neighborhood businesses, public gathering places, and the people who fill them with life.

My priorities

  • Care for neighborhood parks, trails, playgrounds, trees, and public gathering spots.
  • Support reinvestment in storefronts and walkable commercial corridors.
  • Address neglected properties with fair, transparent enforcement and opportunities for reuse.
  • Make space for local arts, recreation, and neighborhood-led projects.
  • Protect what makes District 9 feel like home while planning for its future.
A question for neighbors: Which neighborhood place deserves more attention?
