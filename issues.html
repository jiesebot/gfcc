THE ISSUES

A city that works in real life.

Big ideas matter most when they make daily life better. Here's a more detailed look at the priorities shaping Devan's campaign for District 9.

LET'S GET LOCAL.✳

These are proposed directions for the campaign, not promises that any one council member can deliver alone. City policies require public discussion, budgets, and cooperation.

01HOUSING & AFFORDABILITY

Making room for more neighbors.

Green Bay should offer housing options that work for different incomes, family sizes, and stages of life.

What that could look like

  • Review local zoning and permitting rules that may make new housing more difficult or costly to build.
  • Consider a broader mix of housing types, including smaller homes, apartments, and accessible housing.
  • Encourage responsible development while giving current residents clear opportunities to participate in decisions.
  • Examine how infrastructure investments and development affect existing neighborhoods and affordability.
The question for neighbors: What would make it easier for you or someone you know to find and keep a home here?
02STREETS & ACCESSIBILITY

Safe routes, real access.

Whether you travel by car, bus, bicycle, wheelchair, or on foot, public infrastructure should help you get where you're going.

What that could look like

  • Pay attention to sidewalk conditions, curb ramps, winter maintenance, and safe crossings.
  • Use residents' feedback and available safety data to inform street repair and improvement priorities.
  • Consider how transportation choices connect residents with jobs, schools, services, and recreation.
  • Make accessibility a routine part of public project planning, rather than a late addition.
The question for neighbors: Is there a street, intersection, or public space you find difficult to use safely?
03WORKERS & LOCAL ECONOMY

Respect the people doing the work.

A healthy local economy includes workers, families, and small businesses, not just development headlines.

What that could look like

  • Look at how city procurement and contracting practices affect job quality and public accountability.
  • Support fair treatment of municipal employees and transparent public workforce decisions.
  • Consider the needs of independent businesses and neighborhood commercial corridors.
  • Evaluate economic development proposals for their long-term public costs and community benefits.
The question for neighbors: Where could city decisions better support your work, livelihood, or local business?
04TRANSPARENT GOVERNMENT

A City Hall you can reach.

Residents should be able to understand decisions, raise concerns, and follow what happens next.

What that could look like

  • Share plain-language explanations of major council decisions and their budget implications.
  • Improve how residents learn about public meetings and opportunities to comment.
  • Promote accessible communication and more predictable follow-up on constituent concerns.
  • Make space for people who cannot easily attend a weekday meeting in person.
The question for neighbors: What would make local government more understandable and accessible to you?
